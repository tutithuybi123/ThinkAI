# MVP and demo review

## Three different minimums

| Artifact | Minimum | It proves | It cannot prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical prototype | one micro-skill, 6–10 teacher-reviewed item pairs, 3 fixed hints, isolated trial, symbolic scoring, event log | workflow is operable and traceable | learning benefit |
| Research validation | consented learners, strong tutor control, held-out immediate and delayed outcomes | predictive validity and/or causal learning effect | mass desirability |
| Competition MVP | reliable challenge flow, auditable content source, synthetic/demo accounts clearly marked, one historical timestamped return | a judge can understand and inspect the mechanism | real retention from a live 3-minute session |

The proposed MVP is slightly too large if it includes freeform AI diagnosis and general transfer generation. It is sufficient if narrowed to **one atomic Grade-10 linear-function relation**, fixed authored hints, a fixed reviewed item family, and deterministic scoring. One THPT math *unit* is too broad; one micro-skill is correct.

## Data reality check

| Resource | MVP role | Ground truth / validation | License/leakage/Vietnam fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher-authored THPT micro-skill bank | primary content | teacher-approved targets, pair rubric, worked solutions, symbolic answer checks | strongest fit; record authorship/permission and keep held-out pairs out of tutor context |
| Consented pilot event data | measurement/UX study | independently administered delayed tasks, reviewable scoring | no real student data until consent/retention/deletion process exists; pseudonymize |
| Eedi misconception data | optional research inspiration/error taxonomy only | labels are task-specific and need teacher review | do not assume product-use rights or Vietnamese curriculum fit; it should not define the product |
| LLM-generated candidates | backlog for review | never ground truth; accept only after independent teacher/solver checks | leakage and quality risk; preserve prompt/model/version and rejection reasons |

For a competition prototype, a small, fully attributable teacher-reviewed bank is stronger than a large external dataset. It supplies the only evidence the signature trial needs: that the two tasks test the named relation without accidental difficulty/prerequisite shifts.

## Strong honest 3-minute demo

1. **0:00–0:20:** objective and two identical correct answers. One learner opened a strategy-revealing hint; one did not. Ordinary completion is visibly insufficient.
2. **0:20–1:05:** actual learner flow: submit a first idea; take a fixed hint; solve. Show the event receipt, not a vague “AI score.”
3. **1:05–1:55:** new representation challenge in an isolated screen. The presenter reveals after the response why it is the same relation. Pass creates a “can apply in a new representation” receipt; fail opens a short recovery route without loss.
4. **1:55–2:25:** show a labelled historical, timestamped delayed event from a separate demo account. Do not imply it happened during the presentation.
5. **2:25–3:00:** one teacher/audit view: exact item/hint versions, evidence conditions, and a single slide labelled “validation in progress; not a learning-gain claim.”

An animation may help only after the judge has seen the changed task. The emotional moment is the learner recognizing the same mathematical tool in a new setting, not a dashboard color change.
