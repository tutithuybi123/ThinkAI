## Reason to exist

**A has the strongest reason to exist:** widespread GenAI creates a new measurement failure—assisted output is mistaken for independent capability—and existing tutors generally optimize assisted success rather than verify independence. Its risk is longitudinal/product complexity and overlap with assessment practice.

**B has the cleanest proof:** it asks a bounded question with visible ground truth—does this conclusion follow from these observations? It challenges A because a small held-out experiment can demonstrate the core value without a long user study. It is less broad and may become a feature of A, but that narrowness is also its competition strength.

**C has the strongest immediate consequence:** unsafe crisis information matters. Yet it faces the harshest responsible-AI and deployment burden; authoritative-source delay can make a technically correct system operationally unhelpful.

## Recommendations

* **Candidate A — STRONG FINALIST.** Retains the strongest structural response to a newly AI-created problem. Validate whether independent verification changes learner/teacher decisions.
* **Candidate B — KEEP FOR VALIDATION.** Best challenger and best short technical experiment. Promote only if it beats checklist and generic multimodal-model baselines.
* **Candidate C — KEEP FOR VALIDATION.** Valuable research direction, but do not treat it as deployable safety infrastructure without institutional validation.

Provisional ranking: **A > B > C**. This does not select the final project.
