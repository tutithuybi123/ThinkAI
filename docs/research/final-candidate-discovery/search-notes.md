# Search notes

## Method

This pass began with problem signals across education, information integrity, crisis coordination, accessibility, safety, environmental observation, archives, and maintenance. More than 40% of the explored space was outside generic tutoring. A direction survived only if it had: a specific harmed user; a consequence beyond convenience; an AI-essential unstructured-input step; a credible non-AI baseline; lawful/realistic data; ground truth; a non-chat demo; and a structural distinction from prior art.

Searches emphasized 2024–2026 GenAI adoption/integrity, claim–evidence verification, crisis misinformation, disaster datasets, current products, and contradictory evidence. Scholarly discovery used OpenAlex metadata where available; one anonymous OpenAlex request later hit a temporary rate limit, so no unverified result from that failed request is used.

## Problem signals retained

**FACT:** College Board reported that 84% of surveyed US high-school students used GenAI for schoolwork by May 2025; this establishes rapid adoption, not Vietnamese prevalence or harm ([College Board](https://newsroom.collegeboard.org/new-research-majority-high-school-students-use-generative-ai-schoolwork)). HEPI's 2025 UK undergraduate survey likewise describes widespread adoption and recommends stress-testing assessment ([HEPI](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2025)).

**EVIDENCE:** Research and policy discussion consistently distinguish polished performance from trustworthy learning and scientific integrity; a 2024 *Nature Human Behaviour* perspective describes both opportunities and risks of GenAI for human learning ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02004-5)).

**FACT:** The IAEA reports that synthetic crisis imagery can spread during emergencies and erode trust or divert attention; it emphasizes verification before deployment of AI communications ([IAEA](https://www.iaea.org/bulletin/artificial-intelligence-misinformation-and-emergency-communication)). UNESCO reports that some Vietnamese communities experienced flooding before warnings because of limited near-real-time monitoring capacity; this supports crisis-information importance, not the proposed system's effectiveness ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/strengthening-capacity-viet-nam-early-warnings-disaster-risk-reduction-and-safer-learning)).

## Non-AI adversaries

For B, a teacher checklist can verify required sections and spreadsheet formulas can recompute results. They cannot cheaply link varied handwritten/tabular observations, charts, method text, and prose claims across many submissions. B survives only if multimodal evidence linking materially outperforms the checklist while preserving teacher review.

For C, official alert subscriptions, search, a curated source list, and timestamps solve much value. C survives only for messy forwarded text/image/audio where extracting the actual claim, location, time, and matching authoritative evidence is otherwise slow. It must never replace official alerts.

## Evidence categories

* **Sourced fact:** supported directly by a linked source.
* **Inference:** reasoned conclusion from multiple sources; requires validation.
* **Hypothesis:** proposed causal/user benefit not yet demonstrated.

No user interviews or experiments were conducted, and no benchmark result is claimed.
