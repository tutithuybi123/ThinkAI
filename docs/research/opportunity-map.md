## Current strongest directions (survived the first skeptic pass)

1. **Environmental observation QA** — AI necessity is tangible (anomaly/quality analysis), data can be public/synthetic/collected ethically, and an objective agreement metric is feasible.
2. **School information provenance** — viable only as auditable claim/source analysis with a defined local-language corpus and benchmark, not a fact-check chatbot.
3. **Accessible-material fidelity audit** — real access problem; survives if co-designed with users and evaluated for fidelity/comprehension rather than automatic simplification.
4. **Evidence-constrained misconception diagnosis** — retains a valuable part of THINKAI only after radically narrowing to a curriculum mechanism and delayed-transfer evaluation.
5. **School-route exposure evidence** — strong problem significance and demo potential, but survives only with privacy-by-design and local validation.

## Explicit failure attacks

- A generic AI tutor, therapist, accessibility summarizer, dashboard, or chatbot fails the AI-necessity/novelty tests.
- Clinical mental-health assessment, medical screening, and allocation prediction are rejected for initial scope due to validation, privacy, regulation, and harm risks.
- Every surviving direction still requires stakeholder validation, data-access checks, and a dedicated prior-art search before selection.

## Skeptic challenge of the provisional top five

| Candidate | Disconfirming prior art / barrier | What would let it survive |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental observation QA | GLOBE/iNaturalist already collect observations; AI could merely automate an existing manual rule | A narrow public/local dataset, expert/ground-truth agreement, and proof that an ML baseline beats simple rules |
| School information provenance | Fact Check Explorer/Full Fact already support fact-check workflows; generic chat is a gimmick | Defined local-language corpus and a reproducible claim–source precision/recall benchmark |
| Accessible-material fidelity audit | Immersive Reader and other assistive tools are mature; incorrect transformations can harm access | Co-design with affected users, source-fidelity audit, and comprehension result against a non-AI baseline |
| Misconception diagnosis | ITS/LLM tutors already provide hints, explanations and practice | A restricted misconception taxonomy, teacher-reviewed labels, and delayed transfer/calibration—not satisfaction—outcome |
| School-route exposure evidence | Maps/navigation already optimize routes; risk scores can mislead and expose location data | Privacy-minimal inputs, reviewable hypotheses (not safety promises), and validated segment/intervention outcomes |
