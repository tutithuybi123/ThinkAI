## Most common kill reasons

1. The core need is scheduling, routing, inventory, compliance or workflow state; CP-SAT, graph algorithms, rules, schemas and ordinary software are stronger.
2. The learned layer only summarizes, explains or extracts optional text; it does not change the core outcome.
3. A validated threshold/physics/statistical system is safer and more defensible than learned prediction.
4. The high-stakes setting (clinical, lab, justice, safety, emergency response) requires deterministic controls and accountable human decisions.
5. The apparent product is a generic chatbot, dashboard or upload-to-summary flow with no strong downstream loop.

## Domains that survived particularly well

- Variable visual inspection and real-world condition assessment.
- Accessibility conversion from arbitrary images, scans, speech and video.
- Movement and spoken-performance feedback with reviewable outputs.
- Environmental/citizen-science photo, audio and remote-sensing interpretation.
- Archive cataloguing and handwriting transcription, where human review can create ground truth.
- Selected cybersecurity and semantic document triage where model output routes a human action rather than decides autonomously.

## Domains collapsing under the adversary

- Scheduling, routing, allocation and dispatch.
- Traceability, status tracking, schema/data interoperability and record governance.
- Inventory, household reminders, payment operations and standard compliance workflows.
- Threshold-led safety monitoring and high-consequence clinical/lab workflows.
- Generic service navigation, FAQ and broad administrative assistance.
