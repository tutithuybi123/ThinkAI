## Assessment of the current THINKAI hypothesis

### Reasons to abandon or radically change THINKAI

1. “AI tutor,” chat explanation, quiz generation, Socratic prompts, personalization, and teacher copilot workflows are saturated product and research categories.
2. The PNAS field result and scale-up ITS evidence mean a convincing UI/instant correctness is not evidence of learning benefit.
3. Broad child-facing chat increases privacy, age-appropriateness, hallucination, bias, dependence, and evaluation burden.
4. A generic tutor has no clear objective measure beyond engagement unless it defines a narrow mechanism and delayed-transfer evaluation.

### Evidence that supports retaining parts of THINKAI

1. ITS, self-explanation, retrieval practice, and structured productive failure have evidence; a focused system can build on these mechanisms.
2. AI may be necessary when it interprets a learner’s free explanation or work to select a small diagnostic prompt—a task static content cannot personalize cheaply.
3. A teacher-reviewed, data-minimal misconception/transfer workflow could retain the “help learners think” intent without retaining a generic assistant.

**Verdict:** retain the mission only as a hypothesis. Do not retain the generic product framing.
