# Candidate 04 — Narrow misconception diagnosis with delayed transfer

**Status: BORDERLINE.** It can survive only as a learning-science experiment, not a platform, and only after teacher/consent/evaluation gates are met.

ITS effects are heterogeneous ([Ma et al.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037123); [Kulik & Fletcher](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420)); scale-up is qualified ([Pane et al.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373713507480)). Productive failure and self-explanation are evidence-backed mechanisms, not generic LLM features ([Kapur](https://doi.org/10.1080/07370000802212669); [Bisra et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x)). A high-school field study found GPT-base access aided assisted practice but produced 17% worse unaided performance after removal ([PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)). No Vietnam effect is established.

Prior art includes Khanmigo, ChatGPT/Gemini/Copilot, Duolingo Max, Quizlet Q-Chat, Brainly, Photomath, Socratic, ALEKS, MATHia, Squirrel AI, ASSISTments, IXL, Century Tech, Riiid; historical misconception tutoring exists ([Becker 1988](https://doi.org/10.1080/1355800880250108)).

Use only teacher-authorized synthetic/de-identified explanations with a fixed misconception taxonomy. Baselines: normal practice, static teacher hints, answer/explanation bot, deterministic diagnostic rules. AI: classify diverse free responses and choose teacher-authored minimal prompt. Test held-out label F1/calibration/abstention, next-item accuracy, delayed transfer; success requires a predeclared gain over static hints without harmful false diagnosis. **AI necessity 7/10 conditional.** Kill generic tutor framing; local teachers/students must validate misconceptions/workarounds and consent/data approach.
