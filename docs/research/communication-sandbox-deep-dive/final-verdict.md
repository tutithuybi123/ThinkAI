# Final verdict

1. **Exact human problem:** Students rehearsing a defined school presentation/script lack repeatable, low-stakes practice with timely, interpretable feedback; “no human partner” is a contributor, not a proven primary cause.
2. **Who needs it most:** Secondary/high-school students with teacher-provided presentation tasks, if local interviews confirm the need.
3. **Why AI belongs:** It can supply an on-demand interactive prompt and turn speech into timestamp-linked evidence at a scale a teacher cannot provide. It must not replace the teacher’s judgment.
4. **What current models solve:** Realtime conversation/roleplay, ASR, transcript summaries, basic delivery analytics, and much English pronunciation assistance already work.
5. **What remains difficult:** Valid, fair Vietnamese pronunciation/prosody assessment; robust spoken-debate evaluation; calibration across accents/noise; proving learning benefit.
6. **Objectively evaluable:** Duration, timestamped silences, observed fillers/repetitions, reference-alignment operations, ASR WER and deterministic stability—each with error bounds.
7. **Not objectively evaluable now:** Confidence, charisma, engagement, persuasiveness, broad debate quality/fallacy, and universal “good prosody.”
8. **Vietnamese:** Viable for ASR-linked rehearsal facts and constrained content coverage; not viable for claimed pronunciation/prosody grades.
9. **English:** Viable for narrow assistive read-aloud/pronunciation practice, but a crowded market makes it a poor differentiator.
10. **Training/fine-tuning:** Not now. First test existing ASR/alignment and a teacher rubric against held-out, lawful annotations.
11. **Defensible contribution:** A teacher-scoped, evidence-led Vietnamese rehearsal/retry method that separates measured facts from uncertain suggestions. It is not yet proven novel.
12. **Strongest competitor:** Yoodli overall; VirtualSpeech/Ovation for simulated environments; ELSA/Microsoft for English pronunciation.
13. **3-minute demo:** One Vietnamese scripted presentation, an AI Q&A, audio/transcript-linked evidence, a retry comparison, and a deliberate abstention on an ambiguity.
14. **Anti-cherry-pick evidence:** Frozen held-out clips, WER and operation F1, repeated-run stability, human-rater agreement, subgroup/failure reporting, and predeclared thresholds.
15. **Fit relative to previous candidates:** It is more concrete/testable than the repository’s eliminated generic-tutor baseline, but there is insufficient local evidence to rank it against every previous candidate or to declare it a winner.

The broad “AI Communication Practice Sandbox” should be abandoned. It duplicates established products and risks subjective, unsupported scoring. The evidence-led Vietnamese presentation/script rehearsal reframing is plausible but has not cleared the required technical/teacher validation.
