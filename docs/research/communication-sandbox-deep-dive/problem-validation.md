# Problem validation

## What the evidence supports

Targeted L2 pronunciation instruction and feedback can improve pronunciation outcomes: the 2015 meta-analysis reports positive aggregate effects, while a later meta-analysis cautions that evidence is clearest for targeted monitored production, not global judgments of spontaneous speech ([S02](https://doi.org/10.1093/applin/amu040), [S03](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12345)). A 2024 systematic review found CAPT research concentrated in adult English, read/listen-and-repeat and segmental tasks, with relatively little global intelligibility/comprehensibility work ([S01](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344024000181)).

Therefore the real problem is not proven to be simply “no human partner.” It is **insufficient frequent, low-stakes, targeted practice with timely, interpretable feedback**. Learners commonly use teachers/classes, peers, self-recording, scripts, apps and rehearsals. Scheduling, anxiety, limited teacher attention, fear of error and lack of feedback may each matter, but their relative importance for Vietnamese secondary students remains an untested local hypothesis.

## Target and use-case ranking

| Use case | Evidence-supported need | Evaluation risk | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| English targeted read-aloud / speech rehearsal | Strongest | Moderate; labelled English data/API exist | Real but crowded |
| Vietnamese script/presentation rehearsal | Plausible local need; validate with interviews | Low for transparent delivery facts, high for quality score | Best narrow research survivor |
| English free conversation | Real practice need | Assessment is crowded and grammar depends on ASR | Commodity absent a narrow advantage |
| Vietnamese debate arena | Debate practice is plausible | Oral argument quality/fallacy scoring weak | Do not lead with it |
| “confidence/charisma” coach | User desire plausible | No objective construct | Weak/fabricated if scored |

## Population and framing

Secondary/high-school students practicing a defined school presentation or script are the most feasible first population because a teacher can provide the rubric and reference content. University students and language learners have broader demand but stronger commercial alternatives. This is a communication-training problem with an education delivery context; it should not be marketed as replacing teachers or audiences.

## Explicit uncertainty

No reviewed source establishes that lack of a human partner is the dominant causal barrier, or that a simulated audience improves durable presentation/debate outcomes. Those require local teacher/learner discovery and a pilot. The working one-sentence problem, if retained, is: “Students rehearsing a required Vietnamese presentation cannot repeatedly see transcript-linked, objective delivery evidence and compare a retry without consuming teacher time.”
