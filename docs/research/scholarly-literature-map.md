# Scholarly literature map

This is a curated, bibliographically spot-checked map—not a systematic review. It distinguishes peer-reviewed research from policy guidance. Full-text access was limited to lawful public pages/metadata; DOI links are retained and individual authors/samples should be rechecked before a formal submission.

| Work | Type / stable ID | Method and key finding | Limitation / implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ma et al. (2014), *Intelligent tutoring systems and learning outcomes* | Meta-analysis; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037123) | 107 effects; ITS average gains vary by condition | Tutor category is established prior art, not novelty |
| Kulik & Fletcher (2016), *Effectiveness of ITS* | Meta-analysis; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420) | Positive but heterogeneous controlled-evaluation effects | Constrained ITS ≠ generic LLM chat |
| VanLehn (2011), *Relative effectiveness…* | Review; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369) | Step-based systems can work in narrow conditions | Conversational tutoring is not automatically equivalent |
| Pane et al. (2014), *Cognitive Tutor Algebra I at scale* | Evaluation; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3102/0162373713507480) | Scale-up effects were mixed | Integration/fidelity matter |
| Kapur (2008), *Productive failure* | Classroom experiments; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/07370000802212669) | Problem solving before instruction can aid later conceptual learning | Requires designed consolidation; never equate with abandonment |
| Kapur & Bielaczyc (2012), *Designing for productive failure* | Design research; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10508406.2011.591717) | Structure, comparison and consolidation are key | Operationally demanding |
| Chi et al. (1994), *Self-explanations* | Learning study; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1803_3) | Explanatory links correlate with deeper understanding | Narrow materials/sample |
| Bisra et al. (2018), *Inducing self-explanation* | Meta-analysis; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x) | Self-explanation interventions are useful with variation | Prompts can become compliance |
| Adesope et al. (2017), *Practice testing* | Meta-analysis; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306) | Retrieval practice benefits learning | Does not diagnose why errors occur |
| Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Effective learning techniques* | Evidence review; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266) | Practice/distributed learning rank above rereading/highlighting | Not a product trial |
| Soderstrom & Bjork (2015), *Learning vs. performance* | Integrative review; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000) | Easy-looking performance may not mean durable learning | Measure delay/transfer |
| Aleven & Koedinger (2002), *Learning by doing and explaining* | Tutor evaluation; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2602_1) | Explanatory support can add value | Domain authoring is hard |

## Contradictions and open questions

- ITS meta-analyses show average benefits, while scale-up research warns that implementation changes outcomes.
- Productive failure supports structured initial struggle, not withholding support indiscriminately.
- Immediate success/satisfaction may oppose delayed retention and transfer.
- The most defensible learning research gap is not a universal tutor; it is narrow, locally validated assessment of misconception evidence, explanation quality, or calibrated help timing.
